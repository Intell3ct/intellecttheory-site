So yeah&hellip; I haven&rsquo;t been around much. Not out with people, not online like I used to be.

It started back in April last year. I figured I&rsquo;d bounce back quick. Reasonable timeline, back to normal, back to life. It turned into something longer.

Shoulder impingement, with the joint stiffened up from scar tissue. A stress fracture in my pinky, healing slow. A deep bruise across my upper back. A bulging disc at the base of my spine, L5-S1, with an annular fissure. Problems in my neck. Post-concussion symptoms. And my speech&hellip; changed.

{{< side-photo src="optimized/where-ive-been-xray-800.webp" width="1206" height="1470" alt="X-ray showing a hairline fracture on the pinky finger" caption="Small hairline fracture on my pinky from a retaining wall fall. An incident so small&hellip; lasting ramifications." >}}

I stutter now. Certain words, certain sounds, things I never thought about for a second in my life, get stuck. Talking used to be automatic. Now it&rsquo;s something I work at.

Then the migraines showed up around September. They went away for a while, then came back stronger.

And migraines aren&rsquo;t always a headache. Sometimes it&rsquo;s pressure in my head. Dizziness. Vertigo. Anxiety out of nowhere. Adrenaline surges, heart pounding, can&rsquo;t sleep. I&rsquo;ve been to the ER not knowing what was happening.

I&rsquo;m not telling you this because I&rsquo;m angry. I&rsquo;m not. I&rsquo;ve accepted where I am.

I finished my first round of speech therapy. It helped, and more sessions are on the way. I&rsquo;ve got good people in my corner&hellip; professionals who know what they&rsquo;re doing, family and friends who [show up](showing-up-anyway.html). I&rsquo;m focused on the work in front of me.

The meds help. Some of them. Migraine meds, dizziness meds, nausea meds. Some days are better than others. Recovery isn&rsquo;t a straight line, but I&rsquo;m on it.

## Where I Am Now

More than a year later, I&rsquo;m still working through it.

Neck, spine, shoulder&hellip; still healing. Post-concussion symptoms, the migraine episodes, the dizziness&hellip; still managing. My speech is improving. Not where I want to be yet, but moving in the right direction.

The workers&rsquo; comp case is still going. Working through it with my lawyer while I focus on getting better.

The hardest part? You can&rsquo;t see any of it.

I can look completely fine. Standing right in front of you, looking like nothing happened&hellip; while there&rsquo;s pressure building in my head, the room won&rsquo;t sit still, my heart is pounding, and the words just won&rsquo;t come out right.

But this is happening to me, and I&rsquo;m still living my life as best I can.

Most days I&rsquo;m home. That&rsquo;s just how it is right now. ([Pok&eacute;mon GO](current-addiction-pokemon-go.html) when I can. And this website? Built during all this time home&hellip; took a while, I worked on it when my health allowed.) But when my body lets me, I get out. I see my people. I do what I can do. And I move through all of it mindfully&hellip; listening to my body, knowing my limits, respecting them.

Life doesn&rsquo;t stop. It just looks different for me at this point.
